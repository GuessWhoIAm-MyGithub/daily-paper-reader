Abstract. Few-shot anomaly detection (FSAD) has made significant strides, yet existing methods still face critical challenges: (i) dependence on task- or dataset-specific training/fine-tuning, (ii) reliance on language supervision or carefully hand-crafted prompts, and (iii) limited robust-ness across domains. In this paper, we introduce HyperFSAD, a novel FSAD framework that is training-free, language-free, and robust across domains, offering a powerful solution to these challenges. Built upon DI-NOv3 and a hypergraph-based inference mechanism, our approach per-forms inference without any task-specific optimization or text prompts, while remaining competitive. Specifically, we replace sensitive nearest-neighbor / top-n matching with Sparse Hyper Matching : sparsemax 

first selects the most relevant support patches, which are then aggre-gated into a hyperedge as compact normal evidence to suppress back-ground noise and distractors. We further introduce Dual-Branch Im-age Scoring , which fuses spatial anomaly evidence from the patch-grid anomaly map with global semantic deviation captured by support-aware CLS matching, yielding a robust image-level anomaly score in a strictly visual manner. Notably, all components of HyperFSAD are purely vi-sual, eliminating the need for labor-intensive hand-crafted text prompts. Under the stringent training-free and language-free setting, HyperFSAD achieves state-of-the-art performance across six datasets spanning four industrial datasets (MVTecAD, VisA, MPDD, BTAD) and two medical datasets (RESC, BraTS). 

Keywords: Few-shot Anomaly Detection · Training-Free · Language-Free · Hypergraph 

## 1 Introduction 

Few-shot anomaly detection (FSAD) aims to detect anomalies in images and identify potentially anomalous regions with only a few normal samples available for support. This task becomes especially important in real-world scenarios where data is scarce or pixel-level annotations are expensive to obtain, such as medical image analysis [21,23] and industrial defect detection [27,3]. Existing FSAD methods generally follow two paradigms: source-domain train-ing and target-domain training. Source domain training typically trains a model

In target domain training, a prompt or adapter is learned on the target dataset using only normal samples before testing [19,5,16]. However, in real-world environments, both paradigms face complex and restrictive conditions that hinder training. First, data dis-tributions in real scenarios often change continuously and are hard to model explicitly. For example, lighting fluctuations or material batch differences can introduce implicit distribution shifts [12,10,30], causing training-based methods to degrade during deployment. Additionally, deployment is often constrained by data privacy and cross-domain sharing limitations, making complex re-training or domain adaptation strategies difficult to implement. Taken together, these challenges highlight the limitations of training-dependent FSAD paradigms in real-world environments. In the past two years, numerous CLIP-based anomaly detection methods have emerged [16,2,11,26,32,19,29,20], demonstrating impressive results across various domains. However, these methods either fall into complex prompt engineering or depend on intricate training strategies. These issues inherently limit their flexibility and generality, especially preventing them from transitioning to pure vision-based models such as DINOv3 [28]. This raises an important question: Do we really need vision-language models in FSAD? Furthermore, existing FSAD methods typically use a branch for retrieving the most similar normal sample patch for each patch of the test image [16,19,29]. The similarity is then calculated and converted into an anomaly score. However, this approach is highly susceptible to noise interference, which can lead to inac-curate anomaly detection results. In real-world scenarios, noise or background variations in images may resemble the features of normal samples, leading to false positives and missing true anomalies. For instance, in tumor detection or industrial defect detection, local feature changes may be highly similar to normal regions but do not represent the normal state. This makes single-feature match-ing insufficient for handling complex real-world data. Naturally, some methods have shifted toward top-n matching [20], which considers multiple similar fea-tures and uses multiple comparisons to enhance robustness, aiming to reduce the influence of noise. However, these methods require manually setting the value of n, which can significantly affect the model’s robustness. If n is too small, the model may fail to utilize enough similar features, remaining sensitive to noise and local variations. On the other hand, if n is too large, irrelevant features may be introduced, thereby lowering the accuracy of the classification. Overall, sim-ple retrieving schemes remain inherently sensitive to noise and hyperparameter selection, limiting their robustness and reliability in complex real-world scenar-ios. To address the above challenges, we fundamentally restructure the FSAD paradigm and propose HyperFSAD , a training-free and language-free approach. By removing task-specific training, our framework avoids the practical limi-tations that training-based methods face in real-world industrial and medical environments, where data distributions continuously evolve, anomalies exhibit open-set and non-enumerable characteristics, and deployment is constrained by privacy restrictions. Moreover, being language-free, HyperFSAD eliminates re-liance on labor-intensive hand-crafted text prompts, improving practicality and generalization in real deployments, and enabling us to fully leverage powerful pure vision backbones such as DINOv3. Within a purely visual, training-free setting, a central difficulty in FSAD is that patch retrieval becomes fragile under limited shots: nearest-neighbor matches are easily distracted by background clutter, while Top-n heuristics re-quire a sensitive choice of n. To make retrieval both selective and stable, we propose Sparse Hyper Matching . Instead of committing to a fixed neighbor-hood size, we form hyperedge reconstructions by assigning a sparse, data-adaptive distribution over all support patches using sparsemax [22,25]. This suppresses ir-relevant support patches, reduces noise interference, and stabilizes reconstruction under few-shot supervision. Beyond localization, reliable image-level assessment should not rely solely on pooled local responses, which can be skewed by isolated activations or anomaly-scale variation. We therefore introduce a strictly visual 

Dual-Branch Image Scoring design. The Global Semantic Branch measures deviation from normality via support-aware CLS matching, while the Spatial Ev-idence Branch summarizes evidence from the patch-grid anomaly map. Fusing these complementary cues yields the final image-level anomaly score, without any language supervision or prompts. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: 

– We propose HyperFSAD , a training-free and language-free FSAD frame-work, enabling reliable deployment under distribution shifts, open-set anoma-lies, and strict privacy constraints. 

– We introduce Sparse Hyper Matching , which uses sparsemax to produce data-adaptive sparse weights over support patches for hyperedge reconstruc-tion, improving robustness to background noise without tuning Top-n.– We design a strictly visual Dual-Branch Image Scoring : ¡CLS¿ token comparison for global semantic evidence, and patch-grid anomaly map sam-pling to provide complementary signals and improved robustness. 

## 2 Related work 

2.1 Few-shot Anomaly Detection 

FSAD has evolved rapidly, driven by the need to handle open-world defects with minimal supervision. Early approaches like RegAD [15] adopted registration-based paradigms to align test images with a normal reference, while others, such as FastRecon [5], focused on rapid feature reconstruction to identify devi-ations. Recently, the success of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has spurred a wave of methods like WinCLIP [16], AnomalyCLIP [32], PromptAD [19], and KAG-prompt [29], which leverage CLIP’s zero-shot capabilities by designing text prompts to distinguish normal from anomalous samples. ReMP-AD [20] further enhances this by fusing multi-modal prompts with retrieval mechanisms. Despite their progress, these methods largely rely on either source-domain meta-training [15] or target-domain training [19], and thus inherit the practical con-straints of training in deployment, such as distribution drift, open-set defect emergence, limited compute budgets, and privacy restrictions that hinder fre-quent re-training or domain adaptation. As a result, they fall short of truly training-free deployment. Moreover, their dependence on text prompts often in-troduces a semantic gap when describing complex industrial defects. This issue is especially pronounced when defects are subtle, texture-like, or category-specific, where precise linguistic specification is difficult even for human experts. In con-trast, our work proposes a fully training-free and language-free framework that constructs hypergraph-based feature matches directly from visual embeddings, eliminating the need for parameter tuning or prompt engineering. 

2.2 Hypergraph Learning 

Hypergraphs [31,7,9], which generalize traditional graphs by allowing hyper-edges to connect an arbitrary number of nodes, provide a powerful modeling paradigm for representing high-order correlations among data samples. Unlike simple graphs that are limited to pairwise relationships, hypergraphs excel at capturing complex structural information and have been successfully applied in various computer vision tasks, including image classification [13,8] and object detection [6,18]. In the context of FSAD, standard feature matching often re-lies on simple pairwise similarities ( e.g ., nearest neighbor), which can be highly sensitive to background variations, noise, and support-set outliers, thus under-mining reliability in real environments. Addressing this limitation, we introduce a hypergraph-based approach that leverages sparsemax to construct hyperedges. 3.2 Sparse Hyper Matching 

Most existing FSAD methods localize anomalies by matching each query patch to its nearest neighbor (or the Top-n neighbors) among normal support patches and converting the resulting similarity into an anomaly score. However, nearest-neighbor matching is prone to background clutter and noise, while Top-n match-ing introduces a sensitive hyperparameter n: choosing n too small remains noise-sensitive, whereas choosing it too large may include irrelevant patches and de-grade discrimination. 3.3 Dual-Branch Image Scoring 

Although Sparse Hyper Matching (Section 3.2) provides reliable local evidence at the patch level, local matching discrepancies alone are not always sufficient for robust image-level anomaly assessment. In particular, the anomaly map derived from patch reconstruction is effective for localizing abnormal regions, but its pooled response may still be affected by isolated noisy activations, background clutter, or variations in anomaly scale. Therefore, image-level prediction should incorporate not only local patch evidence, but also a complementary global se-mantic cue. ## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Settings Datasets. Experiments are conducted on six real-world datasets spanning in-dustrial inspection and medical imaging. We adopt four industrial anomaly de-tection benchmarks: MVTecAD [1], VisA [33], MPDD [17], and BTAD [24], together with two medical datasets, RESC [14] for retinal lesion detection and BraTS [23] for brain tumor analysis. Notably, only our method follows a strictly training-free protocol: for each test category, we use K normal images as the support set and directly infer anomaly maps and image-level anomaly scores on the query set via our memory-based matching and scoring rules, without any auxiliary data or prompt/adaptor learning. For fair comparison, we reproduce prior methods under their original training settings (source-domain or target-domain training, including prompt/adaptor learning when applicable), following the configurations reported in their papers. 

Evaluation metrics. Industrial Visual Anomaly Detection is evaluated us-ing the area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUROC), the F1-score at the optimal threshold (F1), average precision (AP), and per-region overlap (PRO). For image-level anomaly classification, we report I-AUROC, I-F1, and I-AP, while pixel-level anomaly segmentation is assessed using P-AUROC, P-F1, and PRO. Due to space constraints, we present only I-AUROC and P-AUROC in the main paper, and report results for the remaining metrics in Appendix A. We present the performance for different numbers of the few-shot normal samples, with K set to 1, 2, and 4. 

Implementation details. We use dinov3 vitb16 as the default vision back-bone. All input images are resized to 448 × 448 before being fed into the model. ## 5 Conclusion 

We introduced HyperFSAD , a training-free and language-free FSAD frame-work designed for realistic deployment where distributions evolve, anomalies are open-set, and training or prompt engineering is impractical. Built on a frozen DI-NOv3 backbone, HyperFSAD performs inference purely through visual match-ing and scoring, avoiding task-specific optimization, adapters, or handcrafted prompts. The core of our approach is Sparse Hyper Matching , which replaces brittle nearest-neighbor and top-n retrieval with a sparsemax -based simplex pro-jection that adaptively assigns sparse fusion weights, selecting a compact set of relevant support patches to form a hyperedge reconstruction; this sparsification suppresses background clutter and distractors, yielding stable few-shot localiza-tion without sensitive retrieval hyperparameters. We further propose a strictly visual Dual-Branch Image Scoring mechanism that combines global seman-tics with spatial evidence from the anomaly map. Under the stringent training-free and language-free setting, HyperFSAD achieves state-of-the-art performance across six datasets spanning industrial inspection and medical imaging, remain-ing simple to deploy; ablations validate the importance of sparse retrieval and fusion for image-level assessment. We hope HyperFSAD will encourage a shift toward a purely visual and training-free FSAD paradigm.

## D Effect of Backbone Choice 

In this section, we study the effect of backbone choice on the performance-efficiency trade-off of HyperFSAD. As reported in Table 9, we compare two representative visual backbones, DINOv3-ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14-336 , on MVTecAD under 1-shot, 2-shot, and 4-shot settings, using image-level AUROC (I-AUROC), pixel-level AUROC (P-AUROC), and inference time as evaluation metrics. Overall, DINOv3-ViT-B/16 consistently achieves better anomaly detec-tion performance than ViT-L/14-336 across all shot settings, while also requiring substantially less inference time. Specifically, under the 1-shot setting, DINOv3-ViT-B/16 improves I-AUROC/P-AUROC from 95.4%/95.7% to 96.3%/97.1%, while reducing inference time from 106.4 ms to 52.6 ms. Under the 2-shot set-ting, it further improves performance from 95.8%/96.1% to 96.8%/97.4%, with inference time reduced from 106.6 ms to 53.1 ms. Similar gains are maintained un-der the 4-shot setting, where DINOv3-ViT-B/16 achieves 96.9% I-AUROC and 97.7% P-AUROC, compared with 96.0% and 96.5% for ViT-L/14-336, while requiring only about half the inference time (59.3 ms vs. 118.9 ms). These re-sults indicate that DINOv3 provides more discriminative and efficient visual representations for our training-free and language-free FSAD framework.
