# Only Train Once: Uncertainty-Aware One-Class Learning for Face Authenticity Detection 

Qingchao Jiang Senior Member, IEEE , Zhenxuan Hou, Zhiying Zhu, Zhenxing Qian, Senior Member, IEEE , Xinpeng Zhang, Member, IEEE , Zaiwang Gu 

Abstract —The rapid evolution of generative paradigms has enabled the creation of highly realistic imagery, which escalating the risks of identity fraud and the dissemination of disinfor-mation. Most existing approaches frame face forgery detection as a fully supervised binary classification problem. Consequently, these models typically exhibit significant performance decay when tasked with detecting forgeries from previously unseen generative paradigms. Furthermore, these methods focus exclusively on either DeepFakes or fully synthesized faces, thereby failing to provide a generalized framework for universal face forgery detection. In this paper, we address this challenge by introducing FADNet (Face Authenticity Detector Net), which reformulates face forgery detection as a one-class classification (OCC) task. By training exclusively on authentic facial data to capture their intrinsic representations, FADNet flags any image whose feature embedding deviates significantly from the learned distribution of real faces as a forgery. The framework incorporates Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) to quantify predictive uncertainty and utilizes a plug-and-play pseudo-forgery image generator (PFIG) to tighten decision boundaries around authentic data. Extensive experimental evaluations on the DF40 and ASFD benchmarks demonstrate that FADNet achieves superior performance and generalization capabilities. Specifically, FADNet substantially outperforms existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods, yielding a remarkable average accuracy of 96.63% and an average precision of 98.83%. Our code are available at https://github. com/hzx111621/Only-Train-Once. 

Index Terms —Face Authenticity Detection, Evidential Deep Learning, Deepfake, Synthetic Face Detection.

Given that generative paradigms will continue to evolve alongside advancements in synthesis technology, relying on model-specific fingerprints or reconstruction error for face forgery detection is no longer a viable or sustainable strategy. To solve this issue, in this paper, we try to decouple the detection framework from the architectural dependencies of forgery models. The differences between our method and the others are shown in the Fig. 1. We reformulate face forgery detection as a OCC task, in which manipulated faces are treated as anomalous samples. Consequently, the objective shifts from detecting forgeries to Face Authenticity Detection. Building upon this foundation, we propose FADNet. By learn-ing representations exclusively from pristine facial imagery, the framework is capable of effectively discriminating forged samples. Furthermore, we incorporate EDL to quantify the model’s predictive uncertainty by parameterizing a Dirichlet distribution. During training, authentic images serve as in-distribution (ID) data. Utilizing the inherent properties of evi-dential learning, the model naturally assigns higher uncertainty to out-of-distribution (OOD) fake images, thereby facilitating a natural separation between real and forged content. To bolster the discriminative power of FADNet, we also introduce a plug-and-play pseudo forgery image module that transforms pristine faces into pseudo forgery images. These samples act as OOD data during training, guiding the model to tighten the decision boundaries around the authentic data distribution. Comprehensive experimental results demonstrate that our FADNet effectively identifies both DeepFakes and fully syn-thesized facial imagery, consistently outperforming SOTA benchmarks by a significant margin. Notably, our method not only achieves superior performance on existing forgery methods but also maintaining good generalization capabilities against newest synthesis paradigms. By integrating EDL, the model provides calibrated uncertainty scores and significantly mitigates model overconfidence, thereby enhancing the overall reliability of the detection process. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: 1) We reformulate the face forgery detection problem by shifting the focus toward facial authenticity, thereby framing it as a OCC task. 2) We propose FADNet, a novel framework that integrates one-class learning and EDL for face authenticity detec-tion. 3) Comprehensive evaluations show that our approach ef-fectively counters diverse facial forgeries. This section provides a brief overview of face forgery techniques, face forgery detection methodologies, and the Evidential Deep Learning. 

A. Face Forgery 

Recent advances in face forgery can be broadly categorized into two domains: Deepfakes [20] and synthetic faces [21]. Deepfake primarily leverage deep learning frameworks, such as GANs and Autoencoders (AEs), to model the underlying distribution of real facial data, thereby enabling high-fidelity manipulation of facial features. Research into deepfake genera-tion is currently concentrated in four mainstream directions: 1) Face Swap [22], Focused on interchanging identities between two individuals; 2) Face Reenactment [23], Emphasizing the transfer of motions and poses from a source subject to a target; 3) Talking Face Generation [24], Centered on achieving seamless synchronization between lip movements and textual or acoustic content; 4) Face Attribute Editing [25], Aiming for the precise modification of specific facial characteristics within a target image. In contrast, synthetic face generation aims to synthesize photorealistic facial images without relying on specific identity references. This line of research mainly focuses on learning the global data distribution of real faces and generating samples through latent space sampling and decoding. Representative methods are primarily based on GANs and DMs, which have demonstrated remarkable capability in producing high-resolution and semantically consistent facial imagery. By leveraging progressive training strategies, hierarchical feature modulation, and iterative denoising processes, these models achieve fine-grained control over facial attributes and structural details. B. Face Forgery Detection 

Recent research has primarily focused on two avenues to enhance the generalization of face forgery detection: tam-pering traces (including generative model fingerprints) and reconstruction discrepancy. Early studies focused on spatial statistical inconsistencies, such as pixel-level blending arti-facts, illumination inconsistencies, or anomalous color space distributions [28]. Simultaneously, researchers observed that upsampling operations within generative networks inevitably introduce periodic spikes in the frequency domain [29], cre-ating unique ”spectral fingerprints.” Reference [30] further demonstrated that each GAN model possesses a specific intrinsic fingerprint that can be leveraged for image attribution and forensics. However, as generative algorithms increas-ingly prioritize perceptual quality, post-processing techniques (smoothing and resampling) can easily obscure these subtle statistical traces. This results in a significant degradation of detection performance in cross-dataset scenarios. To overcome the limitations of fragile traces, a class of detection frameworks based on reconstruction discrepancy has emerged. Instead of searching for local defects, these methods evaluate the authenticity of an image by verifying its compatibility with the generative model manifold. The core hypothesis is that synthetic images exhibit high recon-struction fidelity within their corresponding generative space, whereas real images cannot be perfectly reconstructed by a generative model. For images generated by Diffusion Models, reference [13] proposed DIRE, which employs a process of inversion followed by reconstruction. By employing the reconstruction residual as a discriminative criterion, DIRE fa-cilitates the effective detection of synthetic faces. Furthermore, reference [16] proposed Model Discrepancy Learning , which constructs multiple reconstruction branches to compare the stability of an image across different reconstruction paths. This allows for the identification of subtle deviations on the gener-ative manifold without the need for explicit fingerprints. How-ever, existing detection methods often suffer from significant performance degradation when encountering unseen generative architectures. And they cannot maintain high performance on both Deepfakes and synthetic faces simultaneously. 

C. Evidential Deep Learning 

Evidential Deep Learning estimates the uncertainty in classification predictions by modeling the Dirichlet distribu-tion of class probabilities. Unlike Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) [26], which indirectly infer predictive uncertainty via weight distributions, EDL explicitly models uncertainty based on Subjective Logic theory and the Dempster-Shafer Theory (DST) [27] of evidence. For a given input xi and K classes, the neural network out-puts an evidence vector ei = [ ei1, e i2, . . . , e iK ]T , where each element represents the model’s support for the corresponding class. To ensure the non-negativity of the evidence, a non-negative activation function is applied to the network’s output, such as ReLU. The evidence vector ei is subsequently utilized to define the parameters αi of the Dirichlet distribution, formulated as:

C. Pseudo-forgery Image Generator 

As modern generative models become capable of synthe-sizing highly realistic facial images that are nearly indis-tinguishable to the human eye, detectors trained solely on authentic samples often lack sufficient discriminative power when encountering fully synthetic forgeries. To address this issue, we designed the PFIG module. This module constructs a series of representative proxy forgeries by applying specific perturbations to real faces. By treating these proxy forgeries as OOD samples during training, the model is forced to tighten its decision boundaries to more closely encompass the real manifold. Unlike conventional data augmentation, which aims to preserve semantic consistency, PFIG deliberately perturbs images to push them away from the authentic face manifold, thereby simulating potential forgery artifacts. Specifically, we design four types of perturbations that effectively assist FADNet in learning a more compact manifold representation for pristine images:

> •

> •

Spatial Sharpness Inconsistency: Mixes blurred and sharpened patches to simulate non-uniformity in clarity across the facial manifold.  

> •

Checkerboard Artifacts: Simulating the signature foot-prints introduced by deconvolutional (transposed convo-lutional) layers. JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 5Original Processed

This section provides a comprehensive experimental evalu-ation of the proposed FADNet. Firstly, we assess the general-ization capability of our method by comparing it against five SOTA baseline methods on two large-scale datasets. Together, these benchmarks encompass over 40 diverse face forgery and manipulation techniques. A. Experimental Setup 

Training Details. Our FADNet is optimized using the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 5×10 −5 and a weight decay of 5 × 10 −4. We employ a cosine annealing scheduler to adjust the learning rate over 100 training epochs, with a batch size of 32. The training dataset comprises 10,000 real faces sourced from the FFHQ dataset. Meanwhile, we uti-lize a PFIG to produce a corresponding set of 10,000 pseudo-forgery faces, resulting in a balanced training distribution. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX5060Ti GPU. 

Datasets. We conduct an extensive evaluation on two promi-nent benchmark datasets: DF40 1 [32] and the Asian Synthetic Face Dataset 2 [16] (ASFD). DF40 is a large-scale dataset

C. Visualizations 

To further validate the efficacy of FADNet’s uncertainty-aware mechanism, we conduct a analysis of its decision logic using Grad-CAM [34] visualizations. As illustrated in Fig. 5, when processing authentic facial images, FADNet’s attention is precisely localized on key biometric regions, yield-ing minimal uncertainty. In contrast, for forged images, the resulting activation maps exhibit a distinct diffusion pattern, with the model’s focus extending toward facial boundaries and background regions, accompanied by a significant surge in predictive uncertainty. This difference effectively demonstrates that the uncertainty-aware mechanism in FADNet provides a more interpretable and physical grounding for complex classification decisions. Additionally, we employ the t-SNE [35] visualization method to project the feature vectors extracted from the final layer of FADNet into a lower-dimensional space. As illustrated in Fig. 6, authentic images are tightly clustered, whereas forged samples exhibit a dispersed distribution. This observa-tion aligns with our expectations, demonstrating that FADNet has successfully characterized a discriminative feature space for authentic imagery.

The synergy between these components allows FADNet to effectively characterize the intrinsic distribution of authentic facial features. To evaluate the individual contributions of different pertur-bation types to PFIG, we conducted a series of systematic ablation studies on the DF40 dataset. The removal of any single perturbation type leads to varying degrees of performance degradation, underscoring the critical role each perturbation plays in the training of FADNet. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose FADNet, a one-class learning and uncertainty-aware method for face authenticity detection. Departing from fully supervised paradigms that rely heavily on diverse forged samples for training, our approach lever-ages exclusively authentic facial data. By integrating pseudo-forgery feature simulation with evidential uncertainty mod-eling, FADNet achieves superior generalization capabilities against previously unseen forgery techniques. Extensive exper-imental results on several large-scale benchmarks, including DF40 and ASFD, demonstrate that FADNet significantly sur-passes current SOTA methods in terms of detection accuracy, cross-dataset generalization, and confidence calibration. Fur-thermore, the uncertainty-driven decision mechanism provides enhanced reliability and interpretability for real-world digital forensic applications. In the future, we will focus on exploring more computationally efficient forgery detection architectures and further enhancing the intra-class compactness of authentic images within the t-SNE.
