## Abstract 

Unlike single-face forgeries, deepfakes in complex multi-person interaction scenarios (such as group photos and multi-person meet-ings) more closely reflect real-world threats. Although existing proactive forensics solutions demonstrate good performance, they heavily rely on a “single-face” setting, making it difficult to ef-fectively address the problems of deepfake localization and source tracing in complex multi-person environments. To address this chal-lenge, we propose the Deep Attributable Watermarking Framework (DAWF). This framework adopts a novel multi-face encoder-decoder architecture that bypasses the cumbersome offline pre-processing steps of traditional forensics, facilitating efficient in-network paral-lel watermark embedding and cross-face collaborative processing. Crucially, we propose a selective regional supervision loss. This in-novative mechanism guides the decoder to focus exclusively on the facial regions tampered with by deepfakes. Leveraging this mecha-nism alongside the embedded identity payloads, DAWF realizes the “which + who” goal, answering the dual questions of which facial region was forged and who was forged. Extensive experiments on challenging multi-face datasets show that DAWF achieves excel-lent deepfake localization and traceability in complex multi-person scenes. 

## 1 Introduction 

The advancement of deepfake technology enables the forgery of complex multi-person interaction scenarios (such as group photos, multi-person meetings, etc.) [ 17 , 44 ]. This greatly enhances the consistency between forged content and the real world, making it difficult for common users to distinguish authenticity [ 2, 4, 30 ]. Therefore, to counter the formidable threat posed by deepfake, pas-sive forensics methods [ 21 , 33 ] attempt to verify authenticity by capturing forgery artifacts. However, relying on black-box detec-tion, existing passive forensics struggle to localize forged faces or trace original identities in complex multi-face scenarios. This in-evitably breaks the chain of evidence, undermining their credibility in both legal contexts and practical deepfake mitigation. Unlike passive forensics focused on post-event detection, proac-tive forensics methods introduce pre-event embedding to protect media provenance [ 3 , 34 , 39 ]. By embedding a covert digital wa-termark into the original image, they enable subsequent signal

As illustrated in Figure 1, researchers have primarily focused on two major directions: (1) ro-bust watermarking, which is designed to survive various distortions for source tracing of digital content [ 12 , 24 , 45 ]; and (2) semi-fragile watermarking, which preserves the watermark under common be-nign distortions but degrades to random guessing under malicious tampering, thereby enabling integrity verification and authentic-ity detection [ 32 , 38 ]. The above methods mainly concentrate on single-face scenarios, which fundamentally limits their practical-ity in complex multi-face scenes. To the best of our knowledge, only FaceSigns [ 26 ] explored the extension to multi-face scenarios in its experiments, yet its naive sequential cropping-and-scaling approach results in computational inefficiency and significantly compromises watermark robustness (as detailed in Section 4.2). In fact, multi-face images introduce two major challenges [ 21 ]: (1) the number of real and fake faces is unknown; (2) compared with the ideal single-face image, the resolution of each face is generally lower, and the pose diversity is greater, leading to a significant degradation in face quality. As shown in Figure 2, statistical analysis of the face resolution of 20K sampled images reveals that most face instances are concentrated in lower pixel-value regions, with their statistical centroid located near (62 .4, 62 .5). Conventional proactive forensic methods distribute watermark signals globally. Under localized deepfake attacks, these diluted signals within the facial region fail to support reliable localization. Moreover, relying on a single global watermark for the entire image fails to distinguish multiple subjects. This highlights a critical challenge: how to achieve source tracing for each face in the image and anchor tracing information to individual facial identities. To address the fundamental shortcomings of existing proactive forensics in complex scenarios, we propose the Deep Attributable

This enables a multi-face parallel architecture to efficiently embed a unique identity watermark into each face. By doing so, our approach successfully anchors tracing information to individual facial identities, effectively combating identity theft in synthetic media. ## 2 Related Work 2.1 Passive Forensics 

Passive forensics techniques achieve detection by analyzing intrin-sic anomalies (such as texture distortion and frequency domain noise) [ 18 , 23 ] and contextual inconsistencies (such as lighting and pose logic) in the forged content. Early representative method FWA [ 20 ] detects forgery by identifying geometric warping traces gen-erated during the face tampering process. SBIs [ 28 ] guides the clas-sifier to learn general forgery features by generating “Self-Blended Images”. With the maturity of deepfake technology, forged content naturally transitioned to complex scenarios, in which multiple faces appear in the same frame, but only a subset of them is manipu-lated. Multi-face forgery datasets such as OpenForensics [ 17 ] and FFIW10K [ 44 ] were proposed to simulate real-world environments with low resolution, diverse poses and unknown forgery propor-tions. S-MIL [ 19 ] designs spatio-temporal encoding instances to model the inconsistencies within and between frames. Lin et al. [21 ] significantly improve detection performance in complex multi-person scenarios by combining multi-face relationship learning (MRL) and global feature aggregation (GFA) to explore inter-facial correlations. However, as these methods cannot be traced back to original identity source and lack credibility, we utilize deep attrib-utable watermarking to achieve source tracing for individual face instances. 

## 2.2 Proactive Defense 

Proactive defense embeds specific perturbations [ 31 ] or signals [ 8, 29 ] into the original content before its release to achieve detec-tion and source tracing. CMUA [ 9 ] proposed a cross-model universal adversarial watermark framework to achieve defense against deep-fake. TAFIM [ 1] utilizes an attention mechanism to fuse domain-specific perturbations, which causes organ deformation or texture destruction during the deepfake generation process. TSDF [ 42 ]synergistically combines two defense strategies (interruption and data poisoning) to address the critical persistence vulnerability in

To address this, we achieve simultaneous source attri-bution and deepfake localization for the first time in multi-face scenarios. We construct a novel multi-face encoder-decoder ar-chitecture, enabling parallel watermark embedding and complex cross-regional collaborative processing. 

## 3 Methodology 3.1 Overview 

We propose a Deep Attributable Watermarking Framework (DAWF), specifically designed for multi-face scenarios that mirror the in-tricacies of real-world proactive forensics. As depicted in Figure 3, the DAWF pipeline is structured into three primary stages. A face detector identifies 𝑁 facial regions in the cover image I𝑐𝑜 . The MFEncoder then simultaneously embeds 𝑁 independent messages into these specific regions, producing the encoded image I𝑒𝑛 . To simulate various real-world scenarios, I𝑒𝑛 is processed through a noise pool to generate the noised images I𝑛𝑜 . These distortions are categorized into common perturbations (e.g., JPEG compression) and malicious deepfake manipulations. The MFTracer recovers ro-bust messages from I𝑛𝑜 for source attribution. Concurrently, the MFLocalizer detects inconsistencies between embedded and recov-ered messages to generate the localization mask I𝑙𝑜 , pinpointing the exact faces modified by deepfake operations.

## 4 Experiments 4.1 Implementation Details 

Our DAWF is implemented by PyTorch [ 27 ] and executed on an NVIDIA A40 GPU. The model is optimized on the WIDERFace [ 37 ]training set using the Adam [ 15 ] optimizer for 100 epochs, with a constant learning rate of 4 × 10 −4 and a batch size of 64. Specifically, the detected facial regions are resampled to 64 × 64 as input to our steganographic kernel. An independent 15-bit message is embedded into each face, yielding a total watermark capacity of 15 × 𝑁 bits for cover images of resolution 1024 × 700 , where 𝑁 denotes the number of detected faces. PSNR, SSIM, and BER are used to evaluate visual

Since existing methods primarily focus on single-face scenarios, no prior work can simultaneously achieve dual forensics (i.e., source tracing and deepfake localization) in multi-face scenarios. Consequently, we conducted separate comparisons with state-of-the-art tamper local-ization and image watermarking methods under various distortion settings. To comprehensively evaluate deepfake localization capability, we employ four representative face-swapping models: (1) SimSwap [2 ], an identity-agnostic framework that achieves arbitrary identity transfer while preserving target attributes via weak feature match-ing; (2) Ghost [ 6], an attention-driven generative model renowned for high-fidelity synthesis and seamless blending of skin tones and complex expressions; (3) MobileFaceSwap [ 36 ], a highly efficient model optimized for mobile deployment using dynamic neural network techniques, introducing unique lightweight degradation patterns typical of real-world, low-resource tampering scenarios; and (4) CSCS [ 10 ], a framework utilizing dual surrogate genera-tive models for explicit identity supervision. Notably, since the original CSCS architecture is limited to single-face swapping, we customized it by integrating face detection and seamless blend-ing modules, thereby enabling us to rigorously test our DAWF in multi-person forensic environments.

## 4.3 Comparison with Localization Methods 

The core advantage of DAWF lies in its unprecedented precision for deepfake localization, a capability driven by the synergistic combination of our deep attributable watermarking, multi-face encoder-decoder, and selective regional supervision loss. To ensure a rigorous comparison, we evaluated DAWF against both passive forensics methods (MVSS-Net [ 5], IML-Net [ 25 ], PIM [ 16 ]) and proactive methods (EditGuard [ 40 ], OmniGuard [ 41 ]) using F1-score and AUC quantified by the IoU between predicted and ground truth (GT) forged face bounding boxes. As reported in Table 3, DAWF consistently outperforms all competing baselines by significant margins, achieving an average F1-score of 0.8667 under malicious distortions. Notably, against the CSCS attack, DAWF achieves an outstanding F1-score of 0.9368. This superiority is further corrobo-rated by the qualitative results in Figure 6: while passive models exhibit severe over-sensitivity to benign distortions like JPEG com-pression (leading to false positives), and other proactive methods produce only blurry outlines or scattered dots under deepfake at-tacks, DAWF precisely pinpoints the manipulated faces with dense red masks. Furthermore, while maintaining this superior localiza-tion performance, our framework ensures a near-zero tracing BER (e.g., 0.0490% under Ghost), whereas all other methods fail to achieve effective dual forensics in multi-face complex environments. In terms of computational complexity (detailed in Appendix A.1), our DAWF framework is highly lightweight, comprising only 9.931M parameters and requiring 13.256 GFLOPs. During the infer-ence phase, the average embedding time is merely 0.0131 seconds per image. This high efficiency demonstrates that DAWF achieves real-time processing capabilities, making it highly suitable for de-ployment in multi-face forensics applications.

## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose DAWF, a novel Deep Attributable Wa-termarking Framework designed to tackle the dual challenge of source tracing and deepfake localization in complex multi-face environments. By integrating our multi-face encoder-decoder ar-chitecture with a selective regional supervision loss, our framework successfully provides a robust and scalable solution for deepfake localization and traceability. Extensive experiments exhibits supe-rior generalizability across diverse unseen datasets and effectively suppresses false alarms under common noise attacks, confirming its practical applicability for real-world digital forensics. Future work will concentrate on further enhancing its adaptability against increasingly complex distortions and novel forgery techniques.
